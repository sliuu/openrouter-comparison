一期一会
Chanoyu, without the ceremony. All of the attention, none of the anxiety.

Traditional tea rooms ask you to leave the world at the door. We'd rather you bring it in — the deadline, the group chat, the friend you haven't seen since March — and let the tea slow it down a little.

Every pot at Koyo is brewed by hand at the right temperature for the right time, by someone who has done it several thousand times. Sencha at 70°. Gyokuro at 55°, for two full minutes. Matcha whisked in front of you, never from a machine.

The room is designed around sitting well: low light, good chairs, a tatami alcove for those who want the floor, and outlets where you'd expect them. The rest is up to you.

How to sit

The room changes
with the day.

One space, three moods. We keep the schedule the same every day so you always know what you're walking into.

No. 01働

Work

Open — noon · laptops welcome

Mornings are for getting things done. Outlets at every counter seat, a fast network, and a tea that lasts three steepings while you finish the thing.

  • Counter and window seats, first come
  • Calls at the standing bar, please
  • Onigiri and dorayaki until 2
No. 02静

Quiet hours

Noon — 3pm · screens away

For three hours every afternoon, we ask that phones and laptops stay in bags. Read. Talk quietly. Watch the tea. It sounds like a rule; it feels like a relief.

  • Tatami alcove opens
  • Koicha served by reservation
  • A shelf of books, no checkout
No. 03集

Gather

3pm — close · tables of 2–6

Evenings get a little louder. Bring people. We push tables together, pour hojicha lattes and matcha tonics, and keep the lights low.

  • Reservations for four or more
  • Tea tasting flights, Thu–Sun
  • Whole room available for events

The space

Wood, paper, clay.
Good chairs.

A former print shop on a quiet alley, stripped back to its brick and rebuilt with hinoki cypress, washi, and a lot of restraint.

Seating 席

38 seats. A 14-seat hinoki counter, window tables for two, a tatami alcove, and a courtyard when the fog lifts.

Sound 音

Acoustic panels behind the washi. Music is quiet and mostly Japanese ambient — Hiroshi Yoshimura most mornings.

Access 路

Step-free entry, an accessible restroom, and floor cushions with back support in the alcove.

Shop 店

Every tea we pour is available in tins by the door. Kyusu, chasen and bowls from the makers we use.

This season

Autumn. The leaves turn;
so does the menu.

Koyo means "red leaves" — the season the tea house is named for. Right now that means aged sencha out of the cellar, chestnut in the sweets, and the roasting drum running most afternoons.

Late light on the counter —
the second steep
sweeter than the first.
