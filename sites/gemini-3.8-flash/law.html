Understanding Michigan Law

Michigan's Complex Auto No-Fault System, Decoded.

Following the monumental 2020 Michigan Auto No-Fault Reform, accident victims face a two-front battle:

1

First-Party PIP Benefits

Medical bills, wage loss (up to 3 years), and attendant care paid by your insurer regardless of fault.

2

Third-Party Pain & Suffering

A lawsuit against the at-fault driver for severe impairment of bodily function, scarring, or wrongful death.

* Harlow & Pierce manages both simultaneous claims to ensure your medical treatment is funded while we pursue maximum liability damages.

In Michigan, you generally have 3 years from the date of the incident to file a third-party lawsuit for bodily injury or wrongful death. However, for First-Party PIP Auto No-Fault benefits, you must give formal notice to your insurance carrier within 1 year of the accident (the strict "one-year-back rule"), or you may lose your medical and wage benefits permanently.
Yes. Michigan follows the modified comparative negligence rule (51% bar). As long as you were 50% or less at fault, you can recover pain and suffering damages from the other driver, though your award is reduced by your share of fault. Furthermore, regardless of fault, your First-Party PIP benefits pay 100% of your allowable medical expenses.
