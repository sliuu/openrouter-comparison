Questions

Straight answers, before you call anyone.

Nothing up front. We handle Michigan injury cases on a contingency basis — our fee comes out of the recovery, and only if we win. We also advance all case costs, including expert fees, medical records, and filing fees. If there's no recovery, you owe us nothing.

Michigan's no-fault system means your own auto insurer generally pays medical bills and wage loss after a crash, regardless of fault. But it does not stop you from pursuing a separate claim against the at-fault driver for pain, suffering, and other non-economic damages — if your injury meets Michigan's serious-impairment threshold. The recent overhaul of no-fault law changed the medical coverage options available, so it's worth having an attorney review your policy before you talk to any adjuster.

This is one of the most common problems we see. We can often connect clients with treating providers who agree to work on a letter of protection — meaning they're paid when the case resolves. We'll also make sure you're using every dollar of no-fault or health coverage available to you before you take on any out-of-pocket cost.

It depends on how long you're treating and whether the case has to be filed. Many claims resolve in six to twelve months. Cases that go into litigation typically take one to three years. We won't rush you into a settlement before your doctors can say what your long-term prognosis is — that's how people get underpaid.

Usually not. The large majority of Michigan injury cases settle before trial. But we prepare every file as though it will be tried, and that preparation is exactly what makes insurers offer fair numbers. If a jury is what it takes, we're ready — and we'll tell you honestly what we expect.

Michigan generally gives you three years to file a personal injury lawsuit, but evidence disappears much faster than that — surveillance video gets overwritten, truck logs get purged, witnesses move. And anything you say to an insurance adjuster in the first few days can be used against you later. A short call costs you nothing.
