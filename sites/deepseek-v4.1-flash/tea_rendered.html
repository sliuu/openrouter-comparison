01 — Philosophy

The tea is traditional.
Everything around it is optional.

Kōyō means autumn leaves — the brief moment when things turn extraordinary before they fall. We opened in a narrow Post Street storefront in 2019 with six seats, one kettle, and a bamboo whisk.

The idea was simple: bring the discipline of a Japanese tea room to a city that never sits still — then quietly remove everything that would stop you from sitting.

侘寂

Wabi-sabi

Beauty in the unfinished. Our bowls are mismatched on purpose; the cracks are glazed in gold.

一期一会

Ichigo ichie

One time, one meeting. Every bowl is whisked when you order it, and never before.

間

Ma

The pause. We keep seats empty so the room can breathe — and so can you.

03 — Ritual

Four minutes, start to finish.

A full tea ceremony takes four hours. Ours takes four minutes — and that's the point. Here's how a seat at Kōyō works, plus a timer so you can take the ritual home.

  1. Choose one tea

    Not a flight, not a sampler. One tea, chosen properly, is the whole idea. Ask the counter if you're unsure.

  2. Sit anywhere

    Seats are unassigned. The window bench fills first; the garden room stays quiet all day.

  3. Steep it yourself

    Your tea arrives with a small kettle, a timer card, and a strainer. Pour at your own pace — we won't hover.

  4. Stay as long as you like

    No time limit. No second-drink minimum. Refills are free until you stand up.

04 — The Room

A café that behaves
like a tea house.

Thirty-two seats across two rooms, plus six stools at the counter. Cedar, rice paper, and a single long window that catches the afternoon light on Post Street. Built for reading, working quietly, or doing nothing at all.

  • Wi-FiYes. The password is on the menu card at your table.
  • OutletsAt every other seat, tucked into the baseboards.
  • Quiet hoursDaily 3–5pm. Screens dimmed, headphones on.
  • CounterSix stools, no laptops — conversation only.
