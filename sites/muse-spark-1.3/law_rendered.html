Michigan No-Fault Insider

Michigan's no-fault law is confusing. We make it pay.

After 2020 reform, your PIP choices affect everything. Insurers count on you picking wrong. We don't let them.

What PIP should cover — even if they deny it:
  • Unlimited or capped medical bills, rehab, attendant care
  • 85% lost wages up to 3 years + household replacement services
  • Mileage, prescriptions, home modifications — we itemize it all
Two claims after a crash — we file both:
  • Third-party claim: Pain, suffering, excess wages if you meet injury threshold
  • Mini-tort (up to $3,000): Vehicle damage & deductible recovery
  • Uninsured / underinsured motorist arbitration — we maximize stacks
Miss this = lose everything:
  • PIP application: ASAP, ideally within 30 days of crash
  • One-year-back rule: sue quickly or lose old bills forever
  • 3-year statute for injury lawsuits — evidence fades faster

FAQ

What Michigan clients ask us daily

How much does it cost to hire you?

Nothing upfront. Ever. We work on contingency — typically 33% only if we win. We advance all costs (experts, filing fees, records). If we don't recover, you owe $0. We'll put it in writing.

How long do I have to file after a Michigan crash?

You have 3 years for a pain-and-suffering lawsuit, but only 1 year back for PIP benefits — meaning delays erase bills. And your PIP application should be filed within days. Call immediately; evidence (dashcam, EDR, witnesses) disappears in weeks.

The insurer already offered me money. Should I take it?

Almost never without review. First offers in Michigan average 7-11x lower than final recoveries we obtain. Once you sign a release, you can't reopen — even if you need surgery later. Let us review any offer free in 24 hours.

What if I was partially at fault or have pre-existing injuries?

You can still recover. Michigan uses comparative fault — your award is reduced, not eliminated, unless you're >50% at fault. And aggravation of arthritis, herniations, or prior injuries is compensable. We prove the crash made it worse with doctors who testify.

Will my case go to trial? How long will it take?

About 94% of our cases settle — many in 4-9 months. Complex or trial cases take 12-24 months. We're trial lawyers, so insurers pay more to avoid us in front of a Wayne, Oakland, or Kent County jury. You'll approve every settlement.
