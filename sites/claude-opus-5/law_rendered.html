Michigan no‑fault, decoded

Two claims, not one.
Most people only file half.

After a Michigan auto crash you may have separate rights against your own insurer and against the at‑fault driver. Missing either one can cost you six figures.

  • PIP / First‑party benefitsMedical care, mileage, replacement services and up to 85% of lost wages — regardless of who caused the crash.
  • Third‑party negligence claimPain and suffering, disfigurement and excess wage loss from the driver who hurt you, when you meet the serious‑impairment threshold.
  • Mini‑tort for vehicle damageUp to $3,000 in out‑of‑pocket vehicle damage recoverable directly from the at‑fault driver.
  • Deadlines that end claimsGenerally one year to claim no‑fault benefits and three years for auto negligence. Wait too long and the door closes for good.

Straight answers

Questions we hear every day.

Nothing up front. We work on a contingency fee, which means our fee is a percentage of what we recover for you — and Michigan law caps that percentage in personal injury cases at one‑third of the recovery. We also advance all case costs: filing fees, records, experts, depositions. If we don't win, you owe us no attorney fee.

For most auto negligence claims, three years from the date of the crash. To claim no‑fault (PIP) benefits, written notice is generally required within one year, and unpaid expenses more than a year old may be barred. Claims against governmental agencies can require notice in as little as 120 days, and minors have different rules. Because the exceptions matter more than the rules, call us before you assume you're out of time.

Not before someone on your side reads the release. Early offers are almost always made before the full extent of your injuries, wage loss and future care is known — and signing a release ends your claim permanently. We'll review any offer at no charge and tell you honestly if it's fair.

To recover pain‑and‑suffering damages from an at‑fault Michigan driver, your injury must involve death, permanent serious disfigurement, or a serious impairment of an important body function that affects your general ability to lead your normal life. Michigan courts have made this a fact‑specific, case‑by‑case question — which is exactly why documentation and the right medical experts decide these cases.

The large majority of our cases resolve without a trial. But we prepare every file as though it's going in front of a jury, because that preparation is what moves offers. If your case does go to trial, we'll have you ready — and you'll never walk into a courtroom without us.

You may still recover. Michigan uses modified comparative fault: your damages are reduced by your percentage of fault, and if you're found more than 50% at fault you cannot recover non‑economic damages. Insurers routinely overstate a claimant's share of fault — challenging that number is a core part of what we do.

It depends on your medical treatment more than anything else. Straightforward claims may resolve in six to nine months; litigated cases involving surgery, disputed liability or catastrophic injury often take 18 months to three years. We'd rather wait for the right number than rush you into a cheap one.
